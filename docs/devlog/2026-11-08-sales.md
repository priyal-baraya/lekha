# Recording and cancelling sales

*2026-11-08 · Phase 4: Sales*

## What I built

- **Sales page, New sale:** pick books into a basket, see the total, optionally date the sale earlier, and complete it.
- **Sales page, Recent sales:** the latest sales with their totals, the books in each, and a way to cancel one with a reason.
- **`services/sales.py`:** `record_sale` and `cancel_sale`, each one transaction.
- **`sale_cancellations` table:** when and why each sale was cancelled.

## What I learned

**A transaction is all or nothing.** Recording a sale touches three tables. If the second book in a sale turns out to be out of stock after the first book's stock has already been reduced, the rollback undoes that too. I proved the test guards this by breaking the rollback on purpose and watching the stock come out wrong.

**"Check, then act" has a gap.** Reading stock, checking it, then writing it leaves a moment where the number could change. Doing the check inside the `UPDATE` (`… WHERE stock_quantity >= 2`) closes the gap: if there aren't enough copies, nothing changes.

**Let the database enforce "exactly once".** Making `sale_id` the primary key of `sale_cancellations` means a second cancellation of the same sale is impossible, even if the application code had a bug.

**Never delete history.** Mistakes are fixed by cancelling and re-recording, so every change to stock can be traced to a sale, a cancellation or a correction.

**My example from the brief, verified:** selling 2 copies of a book with 8 in stock left exactly 6, both in the tests and in the real app. Cancelling put it back to 8, and revenue returned to its original total.
